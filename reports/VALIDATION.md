## 2. Structural calibration where the asset value is observable (Strategy)

Strategy's assets are mostly bitcoin, so asset value ($55,555m) and volatility (35.3%) can be measured directly instead of being inferred from the equity. Calibrating the textbook Merton model to the equity instead gives:

| Calibration | Implied assets | Error vs observed | Implied asset vol | Ratio to observed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| merton_kmv_1y | 36,114 | -35% | 80% | 2.27x |
| merton_all_debt_5y | 36,787 | -34% | 81% | 2.29x |
| merton_all_claims_5y | 43,052 | -23% | 73% | 2.07x |

The equity is more volatile than leverage alone explains (its premium or discount to the bitcoin it owns moves too), so equity-based calibration understates assets and roughly doubles asset volatility. For companies whose assets cannot be observed, this error is invisible.

The two disagree by up to half in either direction: a single year's matrix is a noisy, regime-specific estimate, the cohorts are single starting years, and withdrawn issuers that later default are not counted. Rating-based PDs carry this uncertainty even before any market risk premium.
